Warning Signs for AC Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time and money. Keep an eye out for the following clues:

  • Higher summer bills even though the house still feels warm and humid
  • A hot electrical smell or a tripped breaker whenever the air conditioner kicks on
  • Warm or room-temperature air blowing when the thermostat calls for cooling
  • An iced-over evaporator coil or frozen refrigerant lines
  • Clicking or humming outside with no fan movement
  • Hissing or bubbling sounds that suggest a refrigerant leak
  • Rapid on-off cycling that never brings the temperature down
  • Water dripping from the indoor unit or showing up on the ceiling below

Done Right

The Benefits of Professional AC Repair

Start with the refrigerant. It lives in a sealed, pressurized loop, venting it is prohibited under federal rules, and correct service requires gauges, a vacuum pump, and a recovery machine rather than a store-bought recharge kit. Add capacitors that hold a dangerous charge with the power off and a condensing unit fed by 240 volts, and the risks add up quickly. Then there are the diagnostic traps: an iced coil often points to poor airflow rather than low charge, and topping off a system with an unrepaired leak lets moisture and acid slowly damage the compressor while your money leaks out with the refrigerant.

Professional service sees the full picture, not just the symptom. Our technicians meter electrical parts before replacing them, make charge decisions from measured superheat and subcooling, locate leaks with electronic detectors and dye, and check the filter, blower, and static pressure any time airflow could be the issue. The result is a repair that lasts the season instead of a guess that fails in three weeks. If your system is not worth saving, we will tell you directly, backed by numbers rather than a sales pitch.

Prevention

AC Repair Prevention Tips

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Clear the condensate drain when you first turn on the AC each year
  • Change the filter monthly during cooling season; starved airflow is how coils freeze
  • Book a professional tune-up each spring before the first heat wave finds the weak parts
  • Shade helps, but never enclose the outdoor unit; it needs free airflow on all sides
  • Rinse the outdoor condenser coil each spring and keep vegetation two feet back
  • Keep supply and return vents open; closing rooms raises static pressure on the blower
  • Pay attention to buzzing or clicking when the AC starts, a common warning of a failing capacitor
