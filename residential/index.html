Upfront Pricing

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Effective HVAC service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Wilmington HVAC provides honest, upfront pricing across Wilmington, handling everything from a worn capacitor to a full system replacement. Every job begins with a careful inspection of your equipment, including its age, tonnage, fuel type, refrigerant charge, and duct condition, followed by a plain-English recommendation with the price spelled out before any work starts.

Many HVAC problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Refrigerant seeps out through pinhole leaks in the evaporator coil, blower bearings wear quietly, and a weakening capacitor gives no warning until the compressor won't start on the first hot day. If you notice weak airflow, rising energy bills, ice on the refrigerant lines, or rooms that never reach the thermostat setting, schedule an inspection sooner rather than later. Catching it early usually keeps a repairable issue from becoming a replacement.

Do you know how old your system is? Once an air conditioner or furnace passes the 12-year mark, it's worth planning the next one before the current one forces the issue. Emergency replacements happen on the worst days, in heat waves and cold snaps, when choices are limited and everyone is uncomfortable. Our technicians know how the Wilmington climate wears on equipment and will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is nearing the end.

Preventive Care

Maintenance Plans for Wilmington Homes

Each maintenance visit from Wilmington HVAC is far more than a quick filter swap. The technician cleans the coils, verifies the refrigerant charge, tests capacitors and contactors, inspects the burners and heat exchanger, clears the condensate drain, replaces the filter, and measures static pressure across the ducts. Small problems get caught while they're inexpensive to correct, and your system stays running at full efficiency.

Every maintenance plan we write is a little different. An older furnace needs a closer look than a new heat pump, and a dusty area means condenser coils foul faster. We build spring cooling and fall heating visits around your system, your climate, and how your family uses it, so every tune-up helps keep the system efficient and protects the equipment you've already paid for.

The savings add up quickly. Clean coils move heat with less effort, a correct refrigerant charge takes strain off the compressor, and a fresh filter spares the blower motor. Next to the cost of replacing a system even one year early, a maintenance plan is money well spent.

Right-Sized Systems

Comfort Sized to Your Home's Demand

An undersized system runs nonstop and never catches up on the hottest days, while an oversized one short-cycles, wastes energy, and leaves the house humid. Wilmington HVAC sizes every replacement and new installation to your actual home. That means a proper load calculation based on square footage, insulation, window exposure, and duct layout, rather than simply matching the tonnage of the old unit. For ductless mini-splits, it means matching head capacity to each zone so every room holds its setpoint.

Homes change over time. An addition or a finished basement can overwhelm the single-stage system that once kept up just fine. Modern two-stage and variable-speed heat pumps hold tighter temperatures and use much less energy. If you're torn between a heat pump and a furnace-and-AC pair, or ducted and ductless, we'll explain the real trade-offs in SEER2 ratings, operating cost, electrical and venting needs, and installation work.

Common Problems

The HVAC Repairs Wilmington Homes Need Most

Homes in Wilmington run into a familiar set of heating and cooling failures, and each one calls for a different fix. Wilmington HVAC handles them all: no cooling, air that blows but never gets cold, furnaces that won't ignite or short-cycle, frozen evaporator coils, refrigerant leaks, condensers that hum without starting, thermostats that lose their settings, clogged condensate drains, and mini-splits flashing error codes. Our technicians identify the exact fault and target the repair to the component that actually failed.

Some problems are mostly a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A weak capacitor simply keeps the compressor from starting, but a cracked heat exchanger can leak combustion gases into your living space, and a blocked condensate drain can quietly ruin a ceiling. We match the fix to the failure: capacitors, contactors, and fan motors on cooling systems, igniters, flame sensors, and control boards on furnaces, sealed and repaired ductwork where conditioned air is bleeding into the attic, and refrigerant recovered and recharged after a repaired leak. Whatever you're facing, our team has the training and parts to handle it.
